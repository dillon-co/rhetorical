# Additional speech studies

Checked 2026-10-14 against the texts below. Structural and device labels are teaching interpretations, not claims that the speakers consciously followed six Roman sections. Use original summaries plus short excerpts for Churchill and Jobs; link readers to the complete texts. Do not reconstruct missing ancient wording.

## Winston Churchill — Blood, Toil, Tears and Sweat (13 May 1940)

[Primary speech text, International Churchill Society](https://winstonchurchill.org/resources/speeches/1940-the-finest-hour/blood-toil-tears-sweat/)

**Structure:** Churchill asks Parliament to support his government, reports its formation, explains the wartime urgency, states the policy and goal, and closes by inviting collective effort. Background, justification, and appeal overlap; there is no separate, sustained rebuttal. His famous promise comes late, not at the opening.

**Devices:**

- Accumulation: the four-item sacrifice list makes sacrifice tangible through a compact list.
- Question and answer (hypophora): the question about policy anticipates the audience's need for direction, then supplies it.
- Repeated beginnings (anaphora): the repeated denial of survival begins successive clauses, expanding the threatened loss.

**Practice:** State the cost honestly; ask the question your audience needs answered; close with a shared action.

## Steve Jobs — Stanford commencement address (12 June 2005)

[Prepared address, Stanford University](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says)

**Structure:** A modest opening leads to an explicit three-story roadmap. Each personal episode becomes advice: education and curiosity, dismissal and renewal, illness and priorities. Narrative and supporting case alternate. Setbacks are reframed rather than answered in a separate rebuttal. The closing repeats a borrowed farewell.

**Devices:**

- Metaphor: the dots image turns scattered experiences into a visible pattern.
- Balanced contrast (antithesis): the contrast between success as weight and beginning as lightness makes apparent failure feel liberating.
- Parallel commands: the two closing commands gives the ending a repeatable rhythm. Jobs credits these words to the Whole Earth Catalog.

**Practice:** Tell one concrete setback, show how your understanding changed, and end with two matching commands.

## Julius Caesar — Address before Pharsalus, reported in Civil War III.90 (48 BCE)

[Caesar, Civil War III.90, 1914 Loeb translation](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Caesar/Civil_Wars/3D*.html#90)

The source explicitly identifies its 1914 translation as public domain. This is Caesar's third-person retrospective account of his own address, not a preserved verbatim speech or independent verification of his claims. Label the displayed text **Complete surviving report of the address**. Do not label it a complete transcript.

> When, according to the custom of war, he was exhorting his army to battle, and setting forth his unbroken record of kindness to his men, he particularly reminded them that he could call his troops to witness with what zeal he had sought peace, what negotiations he had conducted through Vatinius in conferences and through Aulus Clodius with Scipio, how at Oricum he had urged Libo about the sending of envoys. He had never, he said, wished to squander the blood of his soldiers or to deprive the republic of either of its armies. After delivering this speech, the soldiers clamouring for action and burning with zeal for the fight, he gave the signal with a trumpet.

**Possible teaching divisions:** (1) goodwill through remembered care; (2) supporting evidence through named negotiations; (3) defense of intention through denial of needless bloodshed. The report does not preserve six distinct sections. The last sentence narrates the response; it is not the speech's conclusion.

**Three moves visible in the report:**

- Ethos: the claimed history of kindness asks soldiers to judge the leader through their own experience. An appeal, not a figure of speech.
- Accumulation: several concrete peace initiatives make his claimed restraint seem repeatedly demonstrated.
- Metaphor: “squander the blood” describes avoidable deaths as a wasteful expenditure. This wording is visible in the translation; do not claim it reproduces his exact spoken phrasing.

**Practice:** Establish care, give specific evidence of attempted alternatives, then explain why action is necessary. Treat the report as interested self-presentation rather than neutral proof of motive.
